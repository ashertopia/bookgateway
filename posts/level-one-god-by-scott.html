I used to read a lot of traditional fantasy growing up. Level One God is not that. It’s slice-of-life LitRPG, and if you go in knowing that, it’s a fun ride.

Level One God
Level One God #1
By Zachary Scott
Aethon Books
November 2025

Brynn is a guy from Earth who got pulled into this world and climbed all the way to god tier. “God” here just means the top tier, S tier. Nobody prays to these people. But something out there is so dangerous that Brynn and the other gods would rather give it all up. So they take a hidden “Prestige” path: give up their power and start over at level one. Right away you know there’s a big bad coming, and it’s scary enough that the strongest people alive would rather start from scratch than face it as they are.

So Brynn starts over at level one, Wood tier. Wood runs from level 1 to 50, and you don’t earn levels with experience points. They’re just granted. There are no character stats either: no strength, constitution, charisma, any of that. This is LitRPG light.

He gets to a town, meets the female lead (there’s obviously some tension there), and goes out to fight. His whole goal is to level up, and mostly you’re watching him do it. Compared to a normal fantasy book, not a lot happens, and everything seems to go his way. For slice-of-life GameLit, that’s kind of the point.

If you want an easy, fun, popcorn read where you don’t have to remember most of the details, this is a good book. I’m not complaining. I’m on the third book as I write this, so I’m enjoying it enough to keep going.

My one worry is the math. By the end of book one he’s about halfway through Wood. That’s extremely fast, since it normally takes a human half their life. But at this pace, I don’t see him getting back to godhood unless the author writes 25 or 30 books, and most series peter out long before that.

Maybe the author gets tired of writing a Wood-tier hero and does a time skip, or some deus ex machina fast-forwards his power, or he wins a big fight he shouldn’t and gets a massive boost. But if he can take on the thing the gods couldn’t face without giving up their power, that cheapens how terrible the threat is supposed to be. It also doesn’t hold up logically.

So do I like it? Yes. I’m enjoying it, but I’ve lowered my expectations. It won’t challenge you, and it isn’t trying to.
